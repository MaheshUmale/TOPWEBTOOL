Refinance Rate Lock Explained

Interest rate volatility can erode your refinance savings before closing if you fail to lock in your rate. A rate lock is a lender's guarantee to hold a specific interest rate for a defined period while your application processes. Understanding how rate locks work, when to lock, and what options exist if rates fall helps you secure the best possible refinance terms and protect your break-even calculations.

How Rate Locks Work

Once you apply for a refinance and receive a Loan Estimate, you can request a rate lock from your lender. The lock period typically ranges from thirty to sixty days, though some lenders offer shorter or longer terms. During this period, your interest rate remains fixed regardless of market movements. If rates rise, you are protected. If rates fall, you may miss out unless you negotiated a float-down option. Rate locks are free at most lenders, though some charge an upfront fee or higher rate for longer lock periods.

When to Lock Your Rate

Timing your rate lock depends on market conditions and your risk tolerance. In a rising rate environment, lock as soon as you have a signed purchase contract or refinance application. In a stable or falling market, you might wait to see if rates improve. Many borrowers lock immediately upon approval to eliminate uncertainty. Float-Down Options

A float-down option allows you to reduce your locked rate if market rates fall before closing, typically for a fee. This protects you from locking too early and missing better rates. Float-downs usually require rates to drop by a specified minimum, such as a quarter or half percent, before the adjustment triggers. For borrowers uncertain about near-term rate direction, a float-down provides downside protection with limited upside sacrifice. Ask lenders to price float-down options alongside standard locks to compare total cost.

Rate Lock Expiration and Extensions

If your refinance does not close before the rate lock expires, you face two choices: extend the lock or reprice at current rates. Extensions often require paying a fee, sometimes called a lock extension or commitment fee, which can be substantial. Delays caused by appraisal issues, document requests, or underwriting bottlenecks increase the risk of expiration. To avoid extension fees, submit all required documentation promptly, respond quickly to lender requests, and schedule appraisals early in the process.

Rate Lock Versus Closing Disclosure

Your rate lock agreement is separate from the Closing Disclosure you receive shortly before settlement. The Closing Disclosure reflects the final terms of your loan, including the actual interest rate, closing costs, and monthly payment. Compare these figures against your original Loan Estimate to ensure no unexpected changes. If the rate differs from your locked rate, contact your lender immediately. Errors can be corrected, but time is critical as closing approaches.

Market Factors Affecting Refinance Rates

Mortgage rates correlate with bond yields, Federal Reserve policy, inflation expectations, and lender capacity. When the Fed raises rates, mortgage rates typically rise. Economic uncertainty often drives rates lower as investors seek safe-haven bonds. Monitoring these trends helps you decide whether to lock early or wait for potential improvement. Remember that timing the market perfectly is impossible, so prioritize securing a rate that meets your break-even criteria rather than chasing the absolute lowest available rate.

Protecting Your Break-Even Analysis

Rate locks preserve the monthly savings calculated in your break-even model. If rates rise and you must reprice, your savings shrink and your break-even point extends. Always build a cushion into your analysis by modeling slightly higher rates than currently offered. This conservative approach ensures your refinance remains viable even if market conditions shift before closing.
