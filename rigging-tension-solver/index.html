Rigging Trigonometry & Angle Reference

Understand how sling angle multiplies tension, how leg count shares the load, and how capacity is derated by angle.

How The Formulas Work

In a symmetric bridle, each leg carries an equal share of the load, but the tension in a leg is larger than its share because the leg pulls at an angle. Resolving the vertical component of each leg gives T = (W / n) / sin(θ), where W is the load weight, n is the number of load-sharing legs, and θ is the sling angle measured from horizontal. The horizontal force at each anchor is H = (W / n) / tan(θ). Sling capacity is also angle-dependent: the derated capacity at angle θ is WLL × sin(θ), matching the standard rigging angle-factor table.

Load Sharing & the 4-Leg Rule

Two- and three-leg bridles can be treated as sharing the load equally when the lift points are symmetric and the slings are the same length. Four-leg bridles, however, are rated on a three-leg share, because real loads and slings are never perfectly even — one leg typically carries more than a quarter, and rigging practice credits a maximum of three legs with the load. The solver applies the three-leg share for a four-leg configuration, and the horizontal force per leg is shown so anchors can be checked against their own capacity.

Troubleshooting & Edge-Case Failure Points

  • Shallow angles multiply tension: at 30° from horizontal, tension is double the per-leg share; at 15° it is nearly four times. The solver warns when the angle drops below the 30° absolute minimum.
  • Asymmetric rigging: if sling lengths or attachment points are uneven, one leg takes more than its equal share. The equal-share model is a floor, not a guarantee — real rigging must verify even loading.
  • 4-leg overestimation: assuming four legs each carry W/4 underestimates tension by up to 33% versus the conservative three-leg share. The solver defaults to the conservative rule.
  • Rated capacity is the working load limit (WLL), which already includes the design safety factor. Tension must never exceed the derated WLL at the actual angle.
  • Angle measured wrong: the rigging angle is measured between the sling and horizontal, not vertical. A "60° sling" in the field is usually quoted from horizontal; verify before entering the value.

Step-by-Step Instructions

  1. Enter the total load weight in pounds.
  2. Select the number of sling legs. Four legs are automatically treated as a three-leg share.
  3. Enter the sling angle from horizontal in degrees (recommended 60° or higher).
  4. Enter the rated capacity (working load limit) per leg in pounds.
  5. Click "Solve Sling Tension" to read per-leg tension, load share, derated capacity, horizontal anchor force, utilization, and the pass/fail safety check.
  6. Review the derating table and the warning banner, then copy the lift plan for the rigging team.
