The Math Behind Your Metabolism

Understand the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, activity multipliers, and the calorie targets derived from them.

How The Formula Works

Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) is the energy your body burns at complete rest just to stay alive — breathing, circulation, and cell repair. This page uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, the most widely validated resting metabolism formula. For men: BMR = 10 × weight(kg) + 6.25 × height(cm) − 5 × age + 5. For women: BMR = 10 × weight(kg) + 6.25 × height(cm) − 5 × age − 161. Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE) multiplies BMR by an activity factor that estimates the energy spent on movement, digestion, and exercise: TDEE = BMR × activity factor.

Activity Multiplier Reference

The activity factor models how much you move on top of your resting metabolism:

Level Factor Example
Sedentary 1.2 Desk job, no exercise
Light 1.375 Exercise 1–3 days/week
Moderate 1.55 Exercise 3–5 days/week
Very active 1.725 Hard exercise 6–7 days/week
Athlete 1.9 Twice-daily hard training

Troubleshooting & Edge-Case Failure Points

  • Unit conversion errors: pounds must be divided by 2.20462 to become kilograms, and feet/inches converted to centimetres (1 inch = 2.54 cm). One wrong unit can shift the result by hundreds of calories.
  • Formula validation range: Mifflin-St Jeor was validated primarily for adults aged roughly 18–80. Extreme ages, very high lean mass, or significant obesity reduce the formula's accuracy.
  • Activity overestimation: most people overstate how active they are. A "moderately active" person who actually exercises twice a week may see weight loss slower than expected.
  • Daily fluctuation: your true metabolism varies with sleep, stress, and temperature; a single figure is a starting point, not a law.
  • Body recomposition: as you lose weight, your BMR falls because there is less body mass to maintain — recompute after every 5–10 kg (10–20 lb) change.

Step-by-Step Instructions

  1. Select your biological sex — this decides which constant ( +5 or −161 ) the Mifflin-St Jeor equation applies.
  2. Enter your age in years and your current weight, choosing kilograms or pounds.
  3. Enter your height in centimetres or feet and inches — the page converts automatically.
  4. Pick the activity level that honestly matches an average week, not your best week.
  5. Click "Calculate" and read BMR, TDEE, the cutting target (−20% of TDEE) for fat loss, and the bulking target (+10% of TDEE) for muscle gain.
  6. Use the Copy button to save your numbers, and recompute whenever your weight changes materially.
