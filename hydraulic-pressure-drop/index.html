Fluid Friction Math & Pipe Reference

Understand velocity, Reynolds number, the Darcy-Weisbach equation, and how friction factor changes with flow regime.

How The Formulas Work

Mean velocity is the flow rate divided by the pipe cross-section: v = Q / (π/4 × D²), with the volumetric flow converted from gallons per minute to cubic feet per second. The Reynolds number Re = v × D / ν compares inertial to viscous forces and decides the flow regime. In laminar flow (Re < 2300) the friction factor is exact, f = 64/Re; in turbulent flow (Re ≥ 4000) it comes from the Haaland approximation of the Colebrook equation, which uses the relative roughness ε/D. The head loss follows the Darcy-Weisbach equation: ΔP = f × (L/D) × (ρ × v²/2).

Troubleshooting & Edge-Case Failure Points

  • Laminar vs turbulent: using 64/Re in turbulent flow under-predicts friction by an order of magnitude, and using a turbulent formula below Re 2300 over-predicts. The modeler switches automatically at the regime boundaries.
  • Viscosity units: centistokes and ft²/s differ by a factor of about 1.08 × 10⁻⁵. Entering 32 cSt as if it were ft²/s collapses the Reynolds number and inflates the pressure drop catastrophically.
  • Fittings and valves: the Darcy-Weisbach model covers straight-pipe friction only. Elbows, tees, and valves add equivalent lengths that can exceed the straight run — account for them separately in the length input.
  • Temperature drift: a cold startup with SAE 30 oil can be several times more viscous than at operating temperature. Model the worst case (cold) as well as the nominal case.
  • Zero or negative inputs: flow, diameter, and density must be positive; length can be zero (no pipe, no drop). The modeler validates inputs and returns a clear error instead of NaN.

Step-by-Step Instructions

  1. Enter the volumetric flow rate in gallons per minute.
  2. Enter the pipe inside diameter in inches and the total straight-pipe length in feet (add fitting equivalent lengths if needed).
  3. Select the fluid — its density and kinematic viscosity load automatically.
  4. Select the pipe material to load a realistic absolute roughness, or type a custom value in feet.
  5. Click "Calculate Pressure Drop" to read velocity, Reynolds number, regime, friction factor, ΔP in psi and kPa, and head loss.
  6. Copy the full parameter and result block to your pump-sizing or design notes.
