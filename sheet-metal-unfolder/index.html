Bend Allowance Math & K-Factor Reference

Learn how the neutral axis, bend allowance, and K-factor determine the exact flat blank size before bending.

How The Formulas Work

When sheet metal bends, the material on the outside of the bend stretches while the inside compresses. Somewhere between the two surfaces a plane keeps its original length: the neutral axis. Its position is set by the K-factor, the ratio of the neutral-axis offset from the inner face to the material thickness. Bend allowance is the arc length of that neutral axis through the bend: BA = θ × (π/180) × (R + K×T). The flat blank is the sum of the straight legs plus the bend allowances: flat = Σ legs + Σ BA.

K-Factor Presets & What They Mean

K-factor depends on the bending method and the radius-to-thickness ratio. A 90° air bend in a V-die on common steel usually lands near 0.44; bottoming pushes the neutral axis outward to roughly 0.33; coining, where the punch and die fully confine the material, drops closer to 0.30. Small radii relative to thickness shift the neutral axis inward toward 0.40, while wipe or hemming operations reach up toward 0.50. These presets give you realistic starting values that you can refine with your own shop tests.

Bending Method Typical K-Factor
Air bend, V-die, R/T ≈ 1 0.44
Air bend, small radii (R < T) 0.40
Bottoming in V-die 0.33
Coining 0.30
Wipe / hemming 0.50

Troubleshooting & Edge-Case Failure Points

  • Leg count mismatch: for n bends you must supply n+1 legs. A 90° angle with only two legs is a one-bend part; a U-channel with three legs and two bends is a two-bend part.
  • Bend angle direction: 90° is the classic included angle for most sheet-metal work. Acute and obtuse angles change the arc length, so every angle is entered in degrees and converted internally.
  • Sharp-bend minimum radius: with air bending, a radius smaller than the material thickness risks cracking on the outer fiber, especially in aluminum and high-strength steel. The tool warns when R < T.
  • Springback shifts the delivered angle: air-bent steel may relax several degrees. Compensate with overbend in the tooling or program, then re-measure the true inside radius, which differs from the punch radius.
  • K-factor changes with R/T ratio: the same material and tooling produce a different neutral-axis position at different radii. Validate one K-factor per radius you use, not one per material.

Step-by-Step Instructions

  1. Enter the material thickness T and the inside bend radius R in inches.
  2. Pick a K-factor preset for your bending method, or type a custom value between 0 and 0.5.
  3. Enter the straight leg lengths in inches, comma-separated, one more leg than the number of bends.
  4. Enter the bend angles in degrees, comma-separated, one per bend.
  5. Click "Calculate Flat Pattern" to see total bend allowance, total flat length, and a per-bend table.
  6. Copy the complete development to your blanking/shearing setup sheet.
