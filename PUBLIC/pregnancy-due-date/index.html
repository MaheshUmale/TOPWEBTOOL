How The Due Date Math Works

Naegele's rule, gestational age, and the trimester and milestone timeline explained.

How The Calculation Works

Naegele's rule estimates a due date by adding 280 days (40 weeks) to the first day of the last menstrual period (LMP). The 280 days represent 266 days of fetal development plus about 14 days from the start of the LMP to ovulation. If you know the conception date instead, the calculator adds 266 days — because conception itself happens about two weeks into the cycle. Gestational age (GA) is counted from the LMP: today's GA in weeks and days is simply the elapsed time since the LMP divided into whole weeks and leftover days.

Trimester & Milestone Reference

The pregnancy is conventionally divided into three trimesters. The first trimester runs from week 0 to the end of week 12 (weeks are counted as completed weeks), the second from week 13 through week 27, and the third from week 28 onward. The milestones table marks widely used clinical checkpoints — the first-trimester screening window around weeks 11–13, the anatomy scan near week 20, the third-trimester start at week 28, and the shift to "full term" at week 37.

  • First trimester: weeks 0–12
  • Second trimester: weeks 13–27
  • Third trimester: weeks 28–40+

Troubleshooting & Edge-Case Failure Points

  • Irregular cycles: Naegele assumes a 28-day cycle. If your cycle is longer or shorter, ovulation shifts, and the LMP-based estimate becomes less precise — an early ultrasound is the reliable correction.
  • Unknown LMP: if the last menstrual period is not remembered, conception-based dating adds 266 days, but recall of conception itself can be uncertain.
  • Weeks vs. months: "16 weeks pregnant" means 16 completed weeks (into the 17th). Mixing months and weeks is a common source of confusion when planning appointments.
  • Due dates are estimates: only about 5% of babies are born on their exact due date; a normal range of 37 to 42 weeks is expected.
  • Future/past dates: the calculator accepts any current date to show gestational age at a chosen point, which is useful for planning scans or leave.

Step-by-Step Instructions

  1. Choose whether you know the first day of your last menstrual period or the conception date.
  2. Enter that date, and confirm the current date (or the date you want to evaluate).
  3. Click "Calculate" to see your estimated due date (LMP + 280 days, or conception + 266 days).
  4. Read your gestational age in completed weeks and days, and which trimester you are in.
  5. Scan the milestone table — past milestones are marked "Reached", and the next upcoming one is highlighted.
  6. Use the Copy button to share your summary with your care team, and always confirm with a healthcare provider.
