Composite Laminate Math & Reference

Understand how thickness, areal weight, fiber volume fraction, and laminate stiffness are derived from the embedded ply data below.

How The Formulas Work

Laminate thickness is the sum of each ply's cured thickness times its quantity: t = Σ(qᵢ × tₚᵢ). Areal weight is the sum of ply areal weights (fiber plus resin): AW = Σ(qᵢ × awᵢ), and part weight is area × AW. Fiber volume fraction is thickness-weighted: Vf = Σ(qᵢ×tₚᵢ×Vfᵢ) ÷ Σ(qᵢ×tₚᵢ). Longitudinal stiffness uses the classical transformed modulus E(θ) = E₁cos⁴θ + E₂sin⁴θ, averaged over the stack.

Troubleshooting & Edge-Case Failure Points

  • Areal weight already includes resin — do not multiply it by fiber density to get weight, or every part will come out heavy.
  • Balanced ±45° plies should be added as a single symmetric pair, or the laminate will have coupling that an unbalanced stack hides.
  • 0° plies carry load only along the fiber axis; a part with all 0° plies is stiff but fragile in the transverse direction.
  • Fiber volume fraction presets assume properly consolidated prepreg; a wet layup can sit 5–10% lower in Vf.
  • Thickness tolerances in production are real (±5% or more on cured ply) — quote weight and stiffness windows, not single numbers.
